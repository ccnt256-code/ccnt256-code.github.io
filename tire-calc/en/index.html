How old are my tires? (DOT code)

Every tire has a DOT code on the sidewall. The last 4 digits tell you the week and year it was made. Type those 4 digits, or paste the whole DOT code.

Enter a code above.

Where to find it

Look on the sidewall near the rim for the letters DOT followed by 10–12 letters and numbers. The date is the last group, often inside a small oval. Example: DOT U2LL LMLR 2319 = week 23 of 2019. The full code is sometimes only on one side, so if the outside shows a short code, check the inner sidewall (a flashlight helps).

FAQ

How do I read the tire age from the DOT code?

The last four digits are week and year. 2319 = week 23 of 2019. Tires made before 2000 used 3 digits; if you see that, the tire is far too old.

How old is too old for a tire?

Rubber hardens and cracks with age even when the tread looks fine. Many automakers say replace at 6 years; most tire makers say 10 years is the absolute limit.

Where is the DOT code on a tire?

Molded into the sidewall near the rim, starting with "DOT". The full date code may only be on the inner side.

How much tire size difference is OK?

Stay within about 3% of the original overall diameter. More than that can cause speedometer error, rubbing, and ABS/stability control issues.

Does a bigger tire make my speedometer read wrong?

Yes. With a 3% larger tire, an indicated 60 mph is really about 61.8 mph.
